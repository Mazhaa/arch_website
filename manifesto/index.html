Manifesto

Architecture is not an isolated object, but part of a larger system. It exists through relationships between people, space, materials and the environment.

These relationships are constantly evolving, producing forces, patterns and interactions that can shape the way we inhabit space. Computation offers a way to explore these relationships beyond what can be anticipated through conventional design alone.

It opens up a space for experimentation, where complexity is not simply controlled, but explored. Ultimately, I see architecture as a continuous dialogue between intention and emergence, between what is designed and what develops through interaction.

Architecture is not only what we design, but what emerges.

Process

My process usually begins with observation as a starting point : a site, a sound, a movement, an environmental or spatial relation becomes the starting point. I then translate these observations into measurable and computational conditions. Through the means of procedural, paramatric and iterative methods, these variables are transformed into geometry and spatial organization.

The resulting form is not an end, it becomes a way of testing the initial conditions and the architectural experience.
